# customer_churn_analytics_project
An end-to-end business analytics project identifying customer churn drivers, predicting retention risk, segmenting customers and translating analytical findings into targeted retention strategies.

Here is my customer churn analytics analytics project designed around a business question: Which customers are most at risk of churn, why are they leaving, and what retention actions should the business prioritize?

Dataset

I used IBM's public Telco Customer Churn sample. The commonly distributed CSV contains 7,043 customers and 21 columns; IBM documents the sample as a fictional telecom company where Churn indicates whether a customer left within the last month. The raw file is intentionally kept in data/raw/ locally rather than presented as an original research dataset. Sources: IBM documentation and IBM's archived GitHub sample repository.

What this project demonstrates

My project demonstrates Python, SQL, R, PySpark, EDA, hypothesis testing, bootstrap confidence intervals, K-Means segmentation, Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, model evaluation, permutation importance, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis, Cox regression, Power BI design, DAX and Excel business summaries.

Honest-results policy

All reported analytical results should be regenerated by running the notebooks. No performance metrics, p-values or business findings are hard-coded into the analysis. The Spark notebook uses the real dataset for processing and a repeated-row synthetic benchmark only to demonstrate distributed mechanics; synthetic rows are never used for business conclusions.

Important limitation

This is a cross-sectional customer snapshot, not a longitudinal customer-month panel. Survival analysis therefore treats tenure as observed time-to-churn for churners and right-censored tenure for retained customers. A production retention system should use dated customer events, monthly snapshots, interventions and post-campaign outcomes.

Resume positioning

Emphasize the business workflow: data preparation → SQL analytics → statistical validation → segmentation → interpretable churn prediction → survival risk → scalable Spark processing → BI recommendations.
